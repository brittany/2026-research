This project developed new computational tools to better understand atomization, the process by which a liquid breaks apart into droplets. Atomization plays a critical role in many technologies that affect everyday life, including fuel injection systems, fire suppression equipment, agricultural spraying, aerial firefighting, and industrial coating processes. Despite its importance, the physical mechanisms that govern atomization are highly complex and difficult to predict. The goal of this project was to combine advanced fluid dynamics simulations with modern methods from geometric and topological data analysis to uncover the physics underlying droplet breakup and to improve predictive models of atomization.

The research team created new algorithms that automatically identify and extract individual breakup events from high-fidelity computer simulations of liquid sprays. These algorithms extract detailed descriptions of droplet shapes and the surrounding flow conditions and organize them into a searchable database. The project also developed efficient methods for representing and comparing complex droplet geometries using geometric and topological descriptors, including the Weighted Euler Characteristic Transform (WECT). New software created through this work dramatically accelerates these calculations, enabling researchers to analyze large collections of atomization events and identify physically similar breakup processes.

A major intellectual contribution of the project is the integration of geometric and topological data analysis with computational fluid dynamics. This combination provides a new way to answer a fundamental scientific question: given the shape of a liquid structure and the surrounding flow conditions, when and how does the structure break-up? By identifying similar events in a large database of simulations, the methods developed in this project provide a pathway toward physics-based reduced-order models that predict the atomization process itself rather than only its final outcome. These advances improve scientific understanding of liquid breakup and create new opportunities for data-driven modeling in fluid mechanics and related engineering disciplines.

The project produced conference presentations, journal publications, and software tools that have been shared with the broader research community. Results were disseminated through national and international conferences, including meetings of the Institute for Liquid Atomization and Spray Systems (ILASS), the American Physical Society Division of Fluid Dynamics, the International Conference on Multiphase Flows, and Algebraic Topology: Methods, Computation, and Science (ATMCS). Research findings were also submitted to leading journals in fluid dynamics and computational science including Computers & Fluids, Atomization & Sprays, and SIAM Journal on Applied Algebra and Geometry.

The broader impacts of the project extend beyond research. The project supported the training of graduate students, undergraduate students, and a postdoctoral researcher in interdisciplinary areas spanning fluid mechanics, scientific computing, geometry, topology, and data science. Students gained experience in research, scientific communication, software development, and conference presentations. The project also contributed to educational infrastructure by supporting high-performance computing resources at Montana State University, helping students gain experience with advanced computational tools.

The team engaged the public through outreach activities such as MSU Science Day, MSU Explore Camp, the Science Olympiad, and the Freshman Research Symposium. These activities introduced K–12 students and the broader community to concepts in fluid dynamics, mathematics, and data science while encouraging participation in STEM fields. Ultimately, the knowledge and tools developed through this project will support the design of more efficient spray technologies that reduce waste, improve performance, and benefit applications ranging from energy systems to wildfire management and manufacturing.
