+ Project: Graph Interpolation
* Accomplishments: Created a small novel algorithm for computing the EMST of points moving in random linear directions in the plane.
* Publications: N/A
* Presentations: N/A
* Next steps: Implementation of this algorithm, or use the geometry of the problem to compute Gabriel Graph of points.
* Impact: EMST and Gabriel Graph's are largely computed through the Delauney Triangulation of a set of points,
  Finding a way to approach this from the other directed could be a way to meaningfully improve running time, and establish more of a connection
  between EMST and Gabrial Graphs.
* Your Role: Researcher
+ Collaborators: Jacob Sriraman, Brittany Fasy, David Millman

+ Project: Directed Topological Distances on Graph Immersions
* Accomplishments: Working towards adapting well known distances on undirected graphs, to directed graphs embedded in a directed metric space.
* Publications: N/A
* Presentations: N/A
* Next steps: Characterize which of these distances are metrics, how they relate to each other, and how they differ from their undirected counterparts.
* Impact: Directed topological spaces are gaining traction as the relevant spaces to deal with numerous physical systems. Using distances as a means
to tell directed graphs apart gives a frame in which to compare directed graphs, and could be a jumping off point to extend to higher orders of abstraction.
* Your Role: Researcher
+ Collaborators: Paul Cornish, Brittany Fasy.

+ Project: K-Theory of Multiparameter persistence modules
* Accomplishments: Understanding and the eventual computation of the K-Spectrum of Multiparameter persistence modules via the Waldhausen Construction
* Publications: N/A
* Presentations: N/A
* Next steps: Specific computation of K_1 of a multiparameter filtration, relating resultant Euler curves of a spectrum to physical attributes of a filtration.
* Impact: MultiParameter persistence is an active area of study and finding a way to reasonably compute such objects would be incredibly valuable
in the whole of Topological Data Analysis. Using K theory to study the structure of such filtrations, gives us a better framework in which to understand them and eventually 
towards effecient computation. 
* Your Role: Researcher
+ Collaborators: Madhav C S, Brittany Fasy, Ryan Grady
